Reading Detective

Four moves that win reading marks. Do one part a day, about 10 minutes each.

1 · Crack the question word

One word in a question tells you what kind of answer gets the marks. If you don't know the word, you can't win the marks, even if you understood the story perfectly.

Tip: before you answer any question, find its question word and ask: what does it want me to do?

2 · Impressions: what is this person like?

An impression is what you think a character is like, as a describing word (curious, brave, bossy, kind…), plus the proof from the text.
She seems ___ because ___.

Read: The Lighthouse Key

Step 1 · Clue → describing word

For each clue, pick the word that says what Maya is like.

Step 2 · Be the marker

The real test asks: What impressions do you get of Maya? Give two impressions, using evidence from the text. (3 marks)

How the real marks work: 3 = two impressions, at least one with evidence. 2 = two impressions with no evidence, or one with evidence. 1 = one impression. 0 = just retelling what happened.

Step 3 · Your turn

Write your own answer about Maya: two different describing words, at least one with proof.

3 marks: Maya seems curious because she wants to “see what you're hiding” at the lighthouse. She also seems brave, because she walks the cliff path alone and laughs when the wind shoves at her.
Other words that would score: observant (she notices the star and Grandad's glance), disobedient or headstrong (told three times, takes it anyway), adventurous.

3 · “Give two ways”: two different clues

Two marks need two different clues. Saying the same clue twice in new words only scores once. And each clue must prove the question's key word.

Question words

Read: The Storm Shed

Nobody used the old shed at the bottom of Mrs Patel's garden. Its door hung crooked, and ivy had crept across the only window like a hand.

On Tuesday, Isla and her little brother Ben were sent to fetch the deckchairs. Ben stopped at the gate. “I'll wait here,” he said, kicking at the gravel.

Isla rolled her eyes and marched on. The door groaned as she pulled it. Inside, the air was cold and smelled of old rain. Something ticked in the darkness: slowly, steadily, like a clock that had been waiting.

She held her breath. The ticking stopped.

“Isla?” Ben's voice floated down the path. “Did you find them?”

She looked at the dusty deckchairs, then at the shadowy corner where the sound had been. “Yes,” she called back. “Coming.” But she didn't move. Not yet.

Answers (for marking)

  1. groaned. Accept “crooked” only with a reason (it hung badly). Not a whole sentence.
  2. 1 mark each, up to 2, for different points: he stopped at the gate · he said “I'll wait here” · he was kicking at the gravel (fidgeting/putting it off). Same point twice scores once.
  3. Points: brave / bold (marched on, went in alone) · impatient with Ben (rolled her eyes) · curious (looked at the shadowy corner, didn't move) · calm / keeps secrets (says “Coming” but stays). 3 = two points, at least one with evidence; 2 = two points without evidence, or one with evidence; 1 = one point. Retelling with no describing word = 0.
  4. Points: the ticking in the darkness, then it stops · nobody uses the shed · ivy “like a hand” · cold air, smell of old rain · Isla holds her breath · the story stops before we find out (“Not yet”). Marked like Q3. Do not accept “it is dark” on its own.
  5. She will go and look in the corner / find what was ticking (1), because “she didn't move. Not yet.” or she looked at the corner where the sound had been (1).
  6. It is old / not looked after / creepy, as if something is reaching or holding on.
