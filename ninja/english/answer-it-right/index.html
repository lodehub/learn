Answer it the way the question asks

You already know the answers. This is about giving each question the kind of answer it wants. Every good answer has three parts:

Point: what I think Quote: a few words as proof Explain: what that tells us

Four kinds of question

Before you write, decide which kind it is. Then you know what the marker wants.

A. Find it and reword it

Clue words
in your own wordswhat is meant byhow does he…what happens
Do this
Find the part of the text. Say the idea in different words. Don't copy.
Marks
2 marks = 2 separate points.
Trap
Copying the passage and just swapping one word.

B. Read between the lines

Clue words
suggestshow do we knowsurprisingwhat do you think
Do this
The answer is not written down. Take a clue from the text and work out what it tells us.
Shape
The text says… (quote) → so this shows…
Trap
Retelling the story instead of saying what it shows.

C. Word detective

Clue words
choice of wordshow does the writer…what does the word suggestlanguage
Do this
Pick a word or phrase. Say what it makes you picture or feel. Link that to the character or mood.
Trap
Quoting a word but not saying why it is a good word.

D. Your view

Clue words
do you thinkdo you agreegive reasons
Do this
Say yes or no (or a bit of both). Then give reasons, each with proof from the text.
Trap
An opinion with no evidence.

Two habits that win marks

1. Look at the marks. A 1-mark question needs one point. An 8-mark question needs several developed points with quotes.

2. Finish with "so what?" After every quote, add: "This makes us picture…" or "This suggests that…"

Spot the kind

Tap the clue word that tells you what to do (it turns yellow). Then choose the kind of question.

Build an answer, one part at a time

Question: How does the writer's choice of words suggest Ursula's feelings as she approaches the school?

The school squatted low within its railed, asphalt yard… The whole place seemed to have a threatening expression… The place was silent, deserted, like an empty prison waiting the return of tramping feet.

Quote Pick a short quote to work with

Explain What does it make you picture?

Point So how does Ursula feel?

Now write your own

Question: How does the writer make the discovery of the violin exciting? Use a short quote.

His heart leapt with a violent pulsation as he read the characters…
At this point a feverish anxiety to re-open this cupboard door took possession of him…

Check yourself

The writer shows John's body reacting. “His heart leapt with a violent pulsation” The word “violent” makes his heartbeat sound powerful and almost out of control, so we feel his excitement too. Also, the phrase “a feverish anxiety” suggests he is so eager, like someone with a fever, that he can think of nothing else. This builds tension for the reader.

Say it in your own words

Three answers for each phrase. One copies, one gets the meaning wrong, one is right. Which is which?

Trick: cover the passage, say the idea out loud as if telling a friend, then write it down.
