Cancelling: a shortcut for percentages and ratios

Do the dividing early, while the numbers are still small.

Why cancelling is allowed

Take 4 × 6 ÷ 3. Two ways to do it:

Multiply first

4 × 6 = 24
24 ÷ 3 = 8

Divide first

6 ÷ 3 = 2
4 × 2 = 8

Same answer. The second way just used smaller numbers. Cancelling is dividing early.

Why dividing top and bottom by the same number is safe

5050 = 1, because anything divided by itself is 1. Multiplying by 1 changes nothing. So when you divide a top number and a bottom number by the same thing, you are only multiplying by 1 in disguise.

Why it works across two fractions

A multiplication like 30100 × 501 is really one fraction: 30 × 50100 × 1. Everything on top is multiplied together and everything on the bottom is multiplied together. So the 50 and the 100 are in the same fraction in the end, and you can cancel them even though they started in different places.

Check with 30% of 50. 50 ÷ 100 = 0.5. Then 30 × 0.5 = 15. Cancelling gives the same 15, just by a tidier route.

The rules

Before multiplying, ask one question: does any number on top share a factor with any number on the bottom? If yes, divide both by it. Cancel first, multiply last.
The trap: only cancel across multiplication.
3 + 63 is not 3 + 2. The top is 9, so it is 9 ÷ 3 = 3. The 3 on top is being added, so it is not a factor of the top.

Ratios work the same way. A ratio share is a fraction (parts ÷ total parts) multiplied by the amount, so it is another fraction × whole number question.

Worked examples

Percentage

35% of 40 → 35100 × 401. Divide 100 and 40 by 20: 355 × 21 = 7 × 2 = 14

Ratio share

Share 56 sweets in the ratio 2 : 5. Total parts = 7. Smaller share: 27 × 561 → 7 goes into 56 eight times → 2 × 8 = 16. Larger share: 5 × 8 = 40. Check: 16 + 40 = 56.

Scaling a recipe

200 g butter makes 8 portions. How much for 12? 128 × 2001 → 8 goes into 200 twenty-five times → 12 × 25 = 300 g.
