How much space?

Volume from scratch. You already know perimeter and area. Volume is the same idea, one step further.

Room 1 of 8

Line, flat, solid

You already know two ways to measure a shape:

Perimeter is the distance around the outside. You measure it with a ruler: a line. Units: cm.

Area is how much flat space it covers. You measure it by counting squares. Units: cm², "square centimetres".

But a box, a fish tank or a swimming pool isn't flat. It has length, width and height. How much space is inside? That's volume.

Predict first

Perimeter uses lines. Area uses squares. What do you think we'll count to measure volume?

Try it: which one is it?

For each job, are you measuring perimeter, area or volume?

Big idea. Perimeter is a line (cm). Area is flat squares (cm²). Volume is solid cubes (cm³). The little 2 and 3 tell you how many directions you're measuring in.

Room 3 of 8

Area, then stack it

Counting every cube is slow. Here's the shortcut, and it uses what you already know.

Try it: stack the layers

one layer (area of the floor)
number of layers (height)
volume
Big idea. The floor of a box is a rectangle, and you know its area: length × width. That's how many cubes fit in one layer. Then times by the number of layers, the height.
Volume = length × width × height

Use it

A box is 5 cm long, 4 cm wide and 3 cm tall. What is its volume, in cm³?

cm³

A box has a floor with an area of 20 cm². It is 7 cm tall. Volume?

cm³

Backwards: a box holds 48 cubes. Its floor is 4 long and 3 wide. How tall is it?

cm

Room 4 of 8

cm³, m³ and litres

Predict first

Picture a giant cube, 1 metre on every side (about as tall as your chest). How many little 1 cm cubes would fill it?

Volume also tells you how much a container holds. A cube 10 cm on every side holds 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000 cm³, and that's exactly 1 litre, a big carton of juice. So 1 cm³ is the same as 1 ml.

Big idea. Volume is the space inside (cm³). Surface area is the area of the outside (cm²). Two boxes can have the same volume and completely different surface areas.

Room 6 of 8

The doubling trap

Here's a cube with sides of 2 cm. Its volume is 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 cm³.

Predict first

Make every side twice as long: 4 cm. What happens to the volume?

Try it: grow the cube

Big idea. Make a shape twice as big and the perimeter doubles (× 2), the area goes up 4 times (× 2 × 2), and the volume goes up 8 times (× 2 × 2 × 2). One direction, two directions, three directions.

Room 7 of 8

Beyond boxes: the loaf of bread

Not everything is a box. But lots of shapes are the same all the way through, like a loaf of bread: every slice is the same shape. These shapes are called prisms.

Predict first

A loaf is 10 cm long. Every slice has the same face, with an area of 80 cm². What's the volume of the loaf?

Try it: a tent (a triangular prism)

area of the triangle end
volume

Triangle area is ½ × base × height, the formula you already know. Then × length.

Use it: an L-shaped prism

This is the end face of a 10 cm long step block. Use your area skills: split the L into two rectangles.

Area of the L?

cm²

Volume of the 10 cm long block?

cm³
Big idea. Any prism: volume = area of the end face × length. All your area skills (rectangles, triangles, L-shapes) now work for volume too.
