Room 4 of 7

Letters that make rules

Letters aren't only for puzzles. They let you write a rule that works for every number at once. You've used one already:

A = l × w

That's the area of any rectangle. One short line covers billions of rectangles. That's algebra.

Here's another. In the league, a win gets 3 points, a draw gets 1, and a loss gets 0. If w is the number of wins and d the number of draws:

points = 3w + d

Try it: run the league table

Patterns that grow

Build squares in a row out of matchsticks. How many sticks do you need?

Try it

matchsticks

Start with 1 grey stick. Every square then adds 3 more: a top, a bottom and a right side. So with n squares you need 3n + 1 sticks.

Too big to draw

How many sticks for 20 squares?

And for 100 squares?

Big idea. The 11+ asks for the "next term". Secondary school asks for the rule: 3n + 1. With the rule, you can jump straight to the 100th term without drawing 99 others. And the best rules come from seeing why the pattern grows, not just spotting that it does.
