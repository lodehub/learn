Week 1: say what words do

This week has one job: stop naming techniques and start saying what words make a reader think or feel. Every sentence you write follows this chain.

  • 1. Quote (1 to 4 words)
  • 2. Effect on the reader
  • 3. So What? Why did the writer choose it?
See a worked example first

Line: The sea swallowed the last of the light.

Quote: "swallowed"

Effect: This makes the sea seem like a hungry animal, so the reader feels the darkness is dangerous.

So What: This suggests the writer wants us to see nature as a powerful force that people cannot control.

Notice there is no technique name. Naming "personification" earns very little. The effect and the So What earn the marks.

Warm-up: naming or analysing?

Monday, 5 minutes

Fiction drill: pick three, analyse three

Monday, 15 minutes

Read the passage once for the story. Then tap up to three words or short phrases that did something to you. Tap again to unselect. A phrase can be up to four words long.

Choose your words first, then write. Changing a choice later can clear what you wrote under it.

Active reading log

Tuesday, 15 minutes

Read a short story, a poem, or a magazine or newspaper article of your choice. Read it once for the story or argument. Then reread and underline three words or phrases that did something to you (on paper, or write them here).

Now choose your favourite of the three and run the chain on it.

Non-fiction drill: same chain, new text

Wednesday, 15 minutes

This writer is arguing a point. Tap up to three words or short phrases that show what the writer wants you to believe or feel.
