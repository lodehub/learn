Story Detective 🔍

Four detective skills for reading questions. One part a day is plenty.

1 · Read to the end

Detective rule: don't stop at the first word that fits. Keep reading to the end of the sentence, and the next one. The answer is often hiding there!

2 · What does it tell us?

Detective rule: stories don't always say it straight out. Look at the clues and ask: so what happened?

3 · Always tick a box

Detective rule: when it says Tick one, your answer must be one of the boxes. If your own word pops into your head, find the box that means the same.

4 · Day detective

Detective rule: for each day, go back to that day's part of the story. Don't guess from memory!

Story Detective: The Missing Socks

On Monday, Ella's red sock went missing from the washing line. On Tuesday, her blue sock went missing too. Ella was puzzled. “Socks can't walk,” she said.

On Wednesday, Ella hid behind the shed and watched. A little brown dog crept into the garden, jumped up and pulled a stripy sock off the line!

Ella followed the dog next door. In its basket were lots of socks: red, blue and stripy. The stripy sock was the softest of them all, so the puppy was fast asleep on it.

Answers (for marking)

  1. confused.
  2. blue (not red: red was Monday).
  3. to see who was taking the socks. This needs her to link “socks went missing” with hiding and watching.
  4. Monday → red sock, Tuesday → blue sock, Wednesday → stripy sock (all three for the mark).
  5. a (little brown) dog / the puppy from next door.
  6. the stripy one. The trap: “red” is the first sock in the list.
  7. Because it was the softest (sock). Accept “it was comfy / soft”.

What to look for: Q3 and Q7 are the “so what happened?” questions; Q6 is the “read to the end” trap; Q4 needs her to go back to each day. If she writes her own word next to a tick-box question, remind her: always tick a box.
