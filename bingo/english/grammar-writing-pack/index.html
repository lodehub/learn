4. Verbs and tenses

Remember: Present tense is happening now (I walk). Past tense has already happened (I walked). Some verbs are irregular: run → ran, go → went.

A. Change each sentence to the past tense.

  1. I jump over the puddle.
  2. We eat our lunch outside.
  3. The bird sings in the tree.
  4. He catches the ball.

B. The present perfect: choose has or have.

Remember: We use has or have plus a verb to show something that finished but still matters now: She has finished her book.
  1. I eaten my breakfast.
  2. The dog dug a huge hole.
  3. They built a sandcastle.

9. Direct speech

Remember: Put speech marks (inverted commas) around the exact words someone says. Punctuation goes inside the speech marks. A new speaker means a new line.
“Where are we going?” asked Tom.
Mum smiled and said, “To the beach!”

A. Add speech marks to each sentence.

  1. Watch out! shouted Sam.
  2. I love chocolate cake, said Priya.
  3. The teacher asked, Who has finished?

B. Write what each person might say. Use speech marks.

  1. A pirate finds a treasure chest.
  2. A girl sees a mouse in her bedroom.

10. Apostrophes

Remember: An apostrophe has two jobs.
Missing letters: don’t (do not), can’t (cannot), it’s (it is).
Belonging: the girl’s coat (one girl), the boys’ ball (more than one boy).

A. Write the short form.

11. Commas in lists

Remember: Use commas to separate items in a list. Put and before the last item, with no comma before it.
I packed a torch, a map, some water and a compass.

Write your own sentence listing four things you would take on holiday.

12. Paragraphs and headings

Remember: A paragraph is a group of sentences about one idea. Start a new paragraph when you change time, place, topic or speaker. A heading tells the reader what a section is about.

Writing tasks are open-ended, so use the checklist and the notes below.

Writing tasks: what to look for

Sentence ladder: each step adds only the new feature asked for, and the sentence still makes sense. Setting: uses more than one sense, with adjectives, an adverb and a preposition. Story: clear beginning, problem, solution and ending; varied openers; new paragraphs for changes of time, place or speaker; consistent tense.
