Say it, then flip it

Five minutes a day on 3 × 6, 3 × 7 and 3 × 8. The answer comes out of their head first. The card only checks it.

Work it out

If the answer won't come, they use a route instead of guessing. Each route ends in the same place, and they'll drop the steps once the answer arrives by itself. Try a route in the game with the “Stuck?” button.

Daily 5

Short and often beats long and once. Two tiny goes a day works better than a big session.

  1. Yesterday's tricky ones first30 seconds. The game does this for you when you've logged the day before.
  2. Count in threes together30 seconds. 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24. One finger up per jump.
  3. Play a round3 minutes. They say the answer out loud, then flip. Misses go back in the pile until they have them.
  4. One surprise question later30 seconds, hours after the game: in the car, at teeth-brushing, waiting for the kettle. “What's 3 × 7?” Getting it cold is the real test.
  5. Tick the trackerThe game does it automatically when a round finishes. Tap a box to change it.

Ground rules

  • A wrong answer isn't a problem. Say the right one calmly, let them say it back, and move on.
  • Guessing is fine. A wrong guess before the flip still helps the memory.
  • Stop while they still like it. Five happy minutes beat fifteen grumpy ones.
  • Only these three facts for now. The ones they know can wait.

Cut-out cards

Print, cut round each dashed line, then fold each card down the middle so the sum is on the front and the answer on the back. Use them away from the screen: in the car, at the table, in a coat pocket.
